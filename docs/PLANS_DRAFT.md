# Commercial usage model — draft, not live billing

The app links to a self-contained interactive plan preview. No subscription is sold and no customer quota is enforced. Its values are a product hypothesis, not a profit forecast: Free = one reel up to 60 seconds per monthly period; €15 = 10 shared editing units; €30 = 20; €50 = 40 proposed. A reel up to 60s uses 1, a Stage/Show up to 10 minutes uses 5. In the premium simulation, a 30-minute video consumes 15 units, not 5. Examples are alternatives from a shared balance, not separate additive allowances. Taxes, payment fees and actual provider costs have not been validated. Long jams need a measured, separately costed allowance.

## What the customer sees

Available reels / long videos, a shared balance and a clear consumption estimate before processing. Reservation while processing; confirmation once the file is delivered; release on failure. Preserve projects when a plan runs out. Offer renewal/upgrade before another expensive job starts, never destroy an in-progress user's editing. Distinguish music attribution (composer/licence text) from subscription usage. No per-token coin jargon is needed. Preview calculations are explicitly labelled simulation, do not persist, and do not grant real entitlements.

## What must be built before paid launch

1. Verified account identity, project ownership and server-side authorization. An anonymous Sandbox cookie is not a billable account. The browser must never be able to edit its own entitlement, invent a payment receipt or reset a period.
2. Versioned plan records with currency, monthly period, duration/resolution/concurrency/storage/processing limits and CPU/GPU features. Bind each active subscription to a verified payment-provider customer ID. Verified, signed webhooks activate, renew, change or cancel entitlements; duplicates must be idempotent. Only an explicit checkout starts a payment. No card collection in our editor.
3. A transactional server ledger (Postgres): accounts, subscriptions, jobs, credit_reservations, usage_events and append-only balance entries. Unique job/operation keys prevent duplicate reserves, settlements and refunds. Reserve under transaction/row lock or conditional atomic update so two simultaneous jobs cannot overspend. Track available and reserved balances separately. Reconcile reservations after crashes and renew only once per billing period. A client refresh, retry or repeated download must not charge again.
4. Before starting: probe original duration, source count, channels, resolution, requested outputs and analysis requirements. Estimate both entitlement units and monetary exposure. Require adequate balance, reject unsupported input sizes, enqueue on the correct CPU/GPU worker. Reserve a bounded amount; if scope grows, ask before exceeding the shown estimate. Avoid cutting a user's render at an arbitrary elapsed second; enforce limits before enqueue, then finish bounded reserved work.
5. Meter actual job stages: transcription/alignment seconds and source minutes; LLM provider/model and request IDs plus reported input/output/cached tokens; allocated billable CPU seconds and GPU model/billable seconds; output duration/frames; bytes stored, transferred and retained. Do not confuse elapsed time, utilized CPU time and provider-billed allocation. Use provider usage responses/invoices rather than estimating tokens from word counts. Persist monetary cost with rate version/currency alongside the measurements. Self-hosted Whisper and detection have compute cost even when they use no API tokens.
6. Preview and editing controls do not consume a new delivered-video allowance. They still need bounded technical limits and a compute budget to avoid unlimited free repeated rendering. Reuse transcripts, thumbnails, analysis, proxy files and unchanged renders. Failed delivery releases the commercial allowance; internally retain the actual wasted compute cost for margin analysis.
7. Validate with real workloads and compare invoice cost with the job ledger. Include payment fees, tax treatment, support, storage/egress, idle capacity, refunds and marketing separately. Only then commit to the 15/30/50 quotas and GPU promises. Feature flags may keep draft plans visible only to testers while real enforcement runs in shadow mode.

## Before the server arrives

Test the plan UI, reservation/failure scenarios, account/payment integration using test-mode payments, and ledger concurrency/idempotency with fake accounts. Do not charge real users, purchase servers or claim measured performance. Next week measure the same export jobs on CPU and GPU, then set allowances from observed costs and margin targets.
